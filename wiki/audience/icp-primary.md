# Primary ICP — The Credible Operator

> Non-technical founders and small-business operators who need a real website to look credible, but can't justify a $10k designer engagement.

The primary ICP is the "Credible Operator" — a founder or operator running a business between $0 and $2M in revenue who treats their website as a credibility instrument, not a creative project. They've outgrown a Notion page or a Carrd link. They've tried Squarespace and produced something they're embarrassed to share. They don't want to learn Webflow. They want a site they can point to in a sales conversation without flinching.

These users do not buy on price; they buy on **time-to-credible**. The job-to-be-done is "make me look like a real company by Friday." Cost is secondary to confidence in the output.

## Profile

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Role | Founder, solo operator, fractional exec, consultant |
| Revenue band | $0–$2M |
| Team size | 1–8 |
| Technical fluency | Low to medium — comfortable with SaaS, allergic to code |
| Site purpose | Sales credibility, lead capture, lightweight content |
| Buying trigger | Closing a deal, launching an offer, raising capital |
| Decision speed | Days, not weeks |

## Language patterns

How the ICP describes their problem, in their own words:

- _"I just need a site that doesn't look like a Squarespace template."_
- _"I want it to look like we hired someone, even though we didn't."_
- _"I tried Framer for a weekend and gave up."_
- _"I need this before my pitch on Thursday."_
- _"I don't want to think about fonts."_

These phrasings appear repeatedly in sales calls and onboarding surveys. They are the canonical entry points for headlines, ads, and landing page copy. See [[voice-guide]].

## Top objections

1. **"I've been burned by AI design tools before."** — Counter with the [[design-first-generation]] framework and side-by-side outputs vs. competitors.
2. **"How customizable is it after generation?"** — Counter with the post-generation editor demo. Most users edit <10% of the output.
3. **"Will it look like every other WikiCreator site?"** — Counter with the design-system-per-brand approach. No two outputs share a system.
4. **"Can I move my domain / export the code?"** — Yes, with caveats. See [[offer-architecture]].

## Buying triggers

- Closing a deal and realizing the prospect will Google the company.
- Launching a new offer and needing a landing page within a week.
- Investor or partner asking for a link.
- Hiring — candidates judge employers by their websites.

## Secondary ICP

Boutique agencies (1–5 people) who white-label WikiCreator to deliver client sites faster. Smaller segment, higher LTV, longer sales cycle. Treated separately in [[growth-thesis]].
