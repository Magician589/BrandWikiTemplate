# Common objections

> The four recurring objections in sales calls and the responses that close them.

Across roughly 200 logged sales calls between November 2025 and May 2026, four objections recur on more than half of all calls. They are listed below in order of frequency, with the response patterns that have closed the most deals.

## 1. "I'm worried the output will sound like AI."

**Frequency:** ~70% of calls.

**Response that works:** Show the prospect three live customer sites and ask them to identify which articles were generated and which were edited by a human. Most cannot tell. Frame the product as "AI structures, you supervise" — the human stays in the loop on review.

**Why this works:** The objection is really about reputation risk, not output quality. Showing real customer sites neutralizes the risk frame.

## 2. "I already have a website."

**Frequency:** ~55% of calls.

**Response that works:** WikiCreator runs alongside the existing site as a `/wiki` subdomain or subdirectory. The marketing site keeps its job; the wiki carries the depth. Most customers keep both for 6+ months.

## 3. "How is this different from Notion or Webflow?"

**Frequency:** ~45% of calls.

**Response that works:** Surface the [[competitive-landscape]] comparison table. The differentiator is content origination — WikiCreator generates structured articles from raw sources; Notion and Webflow expect the user to write.

## 4. "I don't have enough content to fill it."

**Frequency:** ~35% of calls.

**Response that works:** Most users underestimate their source volume by 5×. Walk them through a quick audit: call recordings, old emails, voice memos, slide decks, LinkedIn posts. A typical 7-year operator has 80–120 hours of usable recorded material. Offer a free source audit on the discovery call.
