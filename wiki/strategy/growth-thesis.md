# Growth Thesis

> Land on free generations, expand into paid sites, monetize the Agency tier as the long-tail margin lever.

WikiCreator's growth thesis rests on three sequenced bets. The first is acquisition at near-zero cost via free generation — every free user produces a public artifact that itself becomes acquisition surface. The second is conversion through the publish wall, which currently runs at 11–14% and is the company's single most-instrumented funnel. The third is monetization beyond the operator ICP — specifically, the Agency tier — which trades a longer sales cycle for substantially higher LTV.

The thesis is _not_ enterprise expansion. Selling into companies with procurement teams and legal review is a different product, a different brand, and a different unit economic model. See [[positioning]] for what the brand will not become.

## Funnel

| Stage | Volume (Apr 2026) | Conversion rate | Primary lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free signup | 18,400/mo | — | Founder content + SEO |
| First generation | 14,200/mo | 77% | Onboarding copy |
| Publish wall hit | 9,800/mo | — | Output quality |
| Paid conversion | 1,180/mo | 12% | Pricing page + wall copy |
| Agency tier upgrade | 24/mo | 2% of paid | Outbound, not self-serve |

## Three growth bets

### 1. Founder-led content as the acquisition engine

Mira Vance's content (LinkedIn, X, podcast appearances) drives ~40% of attributed signups in Q1 2026. The bet is that founder-led content is more durable than paid acquisition because it compounds. See [[content-strategy]] [To be defined].

This is the bet most at risk of single-point failure. Mitigation: building a second voice on the team is on the 2026 H2 roadmap.

### 2. Publish wall as the conversion mechanism

The publish wall — the moment a free user attempts to publish their generated site and hits the paywall — is the load-bearing conversion event. Current copy: _"Your site is ready. Pick a plan to publish it."_ Tests in Q2 will explore whether showing the live preview before the wall increases or decreases conversion.

The wall position is non-negotiable from a strategy standpoint: moving it earlier kills the demo experience; moving it later destroys monetization. See [[offer-architecture]].

### 3. Agency tier as the LTV multiplier

Agency tier ARPU is 8.5x Solo tier ARPU and churn is materially lower (5% annual vs. 22% annual). The thesis is that small agencies are under structural margin pressure from AI-enabled solo operators, and the Agency tier is the survival path. See [[competitive-landscape]] for the structural argument.

This requires a different motion: outbound, named-account, ~3-week sales cycle. Currently run by Mira directly. Hiring a part-time sales partner is the next operational step.

## Anti-growth principles

The thesis explicitly rejects:

- **Per-generation pricing.** Would lift short-term revenue, would destroy the brand's positioning. See [[offer-architecture]].
- **Enterprise sales.** Wrong unit economics, wrong product surface, wrong buyer.
- **Marketplace dynamics.** WikiCreator does not host third-party templates. Templates would erode the design-system claim. See [[design-first-generation]].
