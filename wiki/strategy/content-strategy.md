# Content strategy

> Long-form writing on the "structured authority site" category, distributed through Maya's LinkedIn and the WikiCreator wiki itself.

WikiCreator's external content has one purpose: educate the market on a category that does not yet have a name. Tactically, this means a slow, deliberate publishing cadence — one essay per month, plus weekly LinkedIn posts from Maya — rather than the SaaS-blog norm of daily SEO content. The wiki itself functions as the long-tail content layer; every article is a public reference document and accumulates organic traffic over time.

## Cadence and channels

| Channel | Format | Cadence | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder LinkedIn (Maya Chen) | Personal essays, in-product anecdotes | 2–3 posts/week | Maya |
| WikiCreator essays | 1,500–3,000 word essays | 1/month | Editorial lead |
| WikiCreator wiki (own product) | Reference articles | Continuous | AI + editorial |
| Podcast guesting | Interviews | 1–2/month | Maya |
| Paid ads | — | — | — |
| SEO blog | — | — | — |

The absence of paid ads and an SEO blog is deliberate. Both channels were tested in 2025 Q3 and produced negative ROI within the primary ICP. See the test memo in `raw/` for details.

## Topics that work

- Critiques of the "personal-brand landing page" pattern.
- Deep-dives on individual customer wikis (with permission).
- Translations of Wikipedia editorial principles into business contexts.
- Anti-content-marketing essays — paradoxically, these convert.

## Topics that do not work

- Generic AI-website-builder posts.
- "X tips for Y" listicles.
- Anything benchmarking WikiCreator against Webflow head-to-head — comparison is fine in product surfaces, performs poorly as content.
